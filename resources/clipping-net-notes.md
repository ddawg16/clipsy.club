## TikTok

- **Algorithm mechanics:** New clips get an initial test push to ~100-500 people. The algorithm reads watch time, completion rate, replays, shares (strongest signal), comments, and likes to decide whether to expand distribution (1K → 10K → 100K → 1M+). Fast engagement in the first hour matters most.
- **Hook (first 1-3 seconds) is everything.** If viewers scroll past in the first second, the clip is dead. Hook types that work: bold claim, question, action start (open mid-action, not with buildup), pattern interrupt.
- **Ideal length:** 15-30 seconds is the sweet spot (highest completion rates the algorithm rewards). 7-15s works for quick reactions/memes. 30-60s only for tutorials/detailed content — completion rate drops. 60+ seconds is hard to make go viral.
- **Captions are mandatory** — 85% of TikTok users watch on mute. Use large, centered, high-contrast (white text/black outline) captions timed to match speech.
- **Posting cadence:** minimum 1/day, recommended 3-5/day, aggressive 5-10/day. More posts = more shots at going viral.
- **Trending sounds:** boost discovery via the algorithm. Tactic — layer a trending sound at low volume behind your own clip audio to get the boost without changing the content.
- **Hashtag formula:** 4-7 total — 1-2 broad (#fyp, #viral), 2-3 niche, 1-2 currently trending.
- **Engagement boosters:** loop clips (end where they begin so people rewatch), end with a question to drive comments, reply to comments fast, use duets/stitches on trending content.
- **Account setup:** switch to Creator Account (free analytics), keep it public (required for view tracking on bounty platforms), turn on comment notifications.
- **Avoid TikTok/other-platform watermarks** on cross-posted content — TikTok (and YouTube, Instagram) demote clips carrying a competing platform's watermark.
- **Best posting times (EST, general US benchmark):** mornings (6-9 AM) and evenings (7-11 PM) cluster as strong slots across weekdays; test against your own audience.

## Instagram Reels

- **Algorithm signals:** watch time/completion, likes/comments/shares, **saves** (a notably strong signal on IG), and shares-to-Stories. Strong performers get pushed to the Explore page.
- **Format:** 9:16 vertical, 1080x1920, ideal length 15-30 seconds (max 90s).
- **Instagram rewards polish** more than TikTok — cleaner, more "produced" aesthetic performs better here even on repurposed clips.
- **Cross-post Reels to Stories** for extra impressions — don't skip this step.
- **Caption strategy differs from TikTok:** the first line is critical (shown before the "more" cutoff), include a call to action (ask for saves/shares/comments), add value-add text expanding on the clip, put hashtags at the end for a clean look.
- **Hashtags:** 5-10, mixed sizes — roughly 2 broad, 3 medium, 3 niche. Put them in the caption, not the first comment.
- **Posting cadence:** minimum 1/day, recommended 2-3/day, aggressive 4-5/day. IG explicitly says **consistency beats volume** — 1 quality Reel daily beats 5 inconsistent ones.
- **Best posting times (EST):** late morning to early afternoon, roughly 10 AM-2 PM depending on day.
- **Common mistakes:** TikTok watermarks (demoted), low resolution, skipping Stories cross-post, no captions, inconsistent posting.

## YouTube Shorts

- **Key structural difference from TikTok:** Shorts rank in YouTube **search** and have a much longer shelf life (views accumulate for weeks/months vs. TikTok's 1-2 day window). Title/description SEO matters here in a way it doesn't on TikTok.
- **Algorithm prioritizes:** total watch time (minutes, not just views), swipe-away rate, subscriber conversion, and session time (does the Short keep people on YouTube longer).
- **Length:** max 60 seconds. Use the full 60s for content needing explanation (educational, story-driven, tutorials, interview context); keep it to 15-30s for quick reactions/punchy quotes/trend content.
- **Always include #Shorts** in the description/title area for correct categorization — videos over 60s don't qualify as Shorts at all.
- **SEO matters:** optimize titles and descriptions with searchable keywords; this is the main lever unique to this platform for long-tail/evergreen views.
- **Posting cadence:** minimum 1/day, recommended 2-3/day, aggressive 5+/day. Timing matters less than TikTok because of the longer shelf life.
- **Best posting times (EST):** midday, roughly 12-4 PM on weekdays, 9-11 AM weekends.
- **Avoid TikTok watermarks** — same demotion risk as Instagram.

## X (Twitter)

- **Different game entirely:** real-time/trend-driven virality, viral speed measured in hours not days. Algorithm favors replies/quote tweets and conversation over pure watch time.
- **Algorithm priority order:** replies & conversation (heavily boosted), quote tweets, bookmarks (strong save-equivalent signal), then likes/retweets, plus watch time/completion.
- **Format:** landscape (16:9) or vertical (9:16) both work. Max length 2:20, ideal 30-60 seconds — noticeably longer than TikTok's ideal.
- **The tweet text matters as much as the video.** Formula: hook statement + context + call-to-action/question to spark replies. Example hooks given: "This is the craziest take I've heard all week", "Nobody is talking about this and they should be...", "Wait for the end..."
- **Hashtags:** 1-2 max — more looks spammy/desperate on X specifically (contrast with IG's 5-10 and TikTok's 4-7).
- **Trend-riding is time-sensitive:** post while a topic is rising, not after it peaks; speed to post often wins on drama/news content (a 24-48 hour window per the stream-clipping guide).
- **Posting cadence:** minimum 1-2/day, recommended 3-5/day, aggressive 10+/day.
- **Best posting times (EST) are more granular/important on X than other platforms** — multiple windows per day (e.g., Mon: 8 AM/12 PM/5 PM). Timing precision matters more here because virality is trend-tied.
- **Tactics:** turn a viral video into a thread (post video → add context in replies → include related clips → pin best performer); quote-tweet other creators' content with your own take to build visibility; reply/engage in your niche's conversations — X's algorithm rewards accounts that interact, not just post.

## Podcast Clipping

- **Best moment types to clip:** hot takes/controversial opinions, revelations/stories, funny moments, advice/wisdom, drama/beef between guests or hosts.
- **Sourcing workflow:** watch/skim at 1.5-2x speed to find moments faster, slow down when something interesting starts; check YouTube comments for viewer-marked timestamps; use chapter markers if available; monitor X/Reddit for which episodes are already generating buzz; prioritize episodes with celebrity/controversial guests.
- **Editing anatomy of a viral podcast clip:** hook (0-3s, start at the most attention-grabbing moment, NOT the beginning of the conversation) → context via text overlay if needed (who's speaking, what's being discussed) → build to payoff/punchline → clean cut at the peak, don't let it drag.
- **Speaker labels and color-coded captions** for multi-person clips; zoom/punch in on the speaker during key moments; cut filler words and dead air ruthlessly.
- **Freshness matters:** clip new episodes over old ones — recency drives relevance and views.
- **Platform-specific notes:** TikTok wants 15-45s podcast clips with immediate hooks; YouTube Shorts can go up to 60s and rewards title SEO; Instagram wants 15-30s and more polish; X clips should tie into trending topics with strong tweet copy.

## Stream Clipping

- **Applies to:** gaming, IRL, Just Chatting, podcast/talk streams, gambling/casino, music/DJ, cooking, fitness — not just gaming.
- **Sourcing:** watch live during peak hours (evenings/weekends) to catch and clip first; review VODs using chat replay/timestamps; check platform-native clip tools (Twitch/Kick clips already flagged by viewers); monitor the streamer's X/Discord/Reddit for buzz; follow 10-20 creators across categories rather than relying on one.
- **Editing:** start directly at the action (no buildup), include the facecam reaction (often more compelling than the moment itself), add text-overlay context for viewers unfamiliar with the streamer, keep it 15-45 seconds, crop out unnecessary stream UI for mobile, balance audio levels between voice/game/donations, consider showing chat reactions for big moments.
- **Speed matters for drama clips specifically — roughly a 24-48 hour viral window** before a moment goes stale; being first to post often wins.
- **Category viral-potential/competition tradeoffs:** Just Chatting and IRL streams rated "Very High" viral potential at "Medium" competition; podcast/talk streams rated "High" potential but "Lower" competition (less saturated niche); gaming FPS/BR content is "High" potential but "High" competition.
- **Pick a niche you understand** — domain knowledge helps you spot what's actually clip-worthy (a poker player spots gambling highlights others miss).

## General Clipper Fundamentals

*(Synthesized from: How to Become a Clipper, How Much Do Clippers Make, Clipping Side Hustle, Freelance Clipper Guide, Remote Clipping Jobs.)*

- **No followers or equipment required to start** — a phone and a free editing app is sufficient. Platforms track views on your account regardless of follower count.
- **Account requirements to get tracked:** account must be public (private accounts can't be tracked), needs some prior posting history, and ownership is verified via a temporary bio code.
- **Realistic timeline given across multiple articles:** Week 1-2 = setup/learning curve, minimal earnings. Month 1 = finding rhythm, roughly $50-$300 depending on effort. Month 2-3 = building momentum, roughly $300-$1,500. Month 6+ = "serious income," roughly $1,500-$10,000+ for full-time-level effort. Most beginners will not make significant money in month one — this is a skill built over reps.
- **Realistic earnings by weekly time commitment** (consistent across the side-hustle and remote-work articles):
  - ~5 hrs/week (casual): $25-$200/month
  - ~10 hrs/week (part-time): $100-$1,000/month
  - ~15-20 hrs/week (serious): $500-$5,000/month
  - Full-time (50+ clips/week, 3M-20M+ monthly views): $3,000-$20,000+/month
- **What actually moves earnings, in order of impact:** (1) which campaign/bounty you join — a $100/100K campaign is 10x a $10/100K one, so campaign selection matters more than clip quality alone; (2) platform choice — TikTok has highest viral potential for new accounts, YouTube often has higher per-view rates and longer shelf life; (3) posting consistency — daily posting builds algorithmic momentum, gaps hurt reach; (4) skill/quality improving over time (better hooks, better editing, better trend timing).
- **Cross-posting the same clip to multiple platforms multiplies earning opportunity** — this is repeatedly emphasized as one of the single highest-leverage moves: edit once, strip watermarks, adapt caption/hashtags per platform, submit to campaigns on each platform separately.
- **Direct-client/freelance rates** (from the Freelance Clipper Guide, useful as a benchmark for negotiating rates outside bounty platforms): Beginner $25-$50/clip, Intermediate $50-$100/clip, Advanced $100-$250/clip, Expert $250-$500+/clip. Retainer deals typically carry a 10-20% discount off per-clip rate in exchange for guaranteed volume.
- **Common mistakes called out repeatedly across articles:** weak/absent hooks, no captions, inconsistent posting, posting to only one platform, ignoring campaign requirements (unapproved clips don't count), keeping other-platform watermarks on exports, giving up before ~30-50 posts (most accounts need that many reps before finding their groove).
- **Batching workflow recommended for side-hustlers:** dedicate a 2-3 hour block (e.g., a weekend session) to create a week's worth of clips at once — cut all clips first, then caption all, then export all, then schedule/post — rather than doing one clip start-to-finish at a time.

## Tools & Platforms

**Editing tools named specifically:**
- **CapCut** (mobile + desktop, free) — repeatedly named the top overall recommendation across articles: free, no watermark, strong auto-captions, templates, effects. The default "start here" tool for most clippers.
- **DaVinci Resolve** (desktop, free) — professional-grade, no watermark, best color correction/audio tools, but steep learning curve; recommended once you want to level up past CapCut.
- **InShot** (mobile) — fast/simple for quick edits, but the free version has a removable watermark and no auto-captions; positioned as a backup, not primary.
- **VN Video Editor** (mobile, free) — no watermark, multi-track editing, more control than CapCut but no auto-captions and a steeper learning curve.
- **Shotcut** (desktop, free, open-source) — budget option, dated interface, mentioned only as a fallback.
- **Adobe Premiere Pro** ($22.99/mo) and **Final Cut Pro** ($299 one-time, Mac only) — called "generally overkill" for clipping specifically; only worth it for complex edits or client work requiring native project files.
- **Recommended progression:** Beginner → CapCut mobile. Intermediate → CapCut desktop + mobile combo. Advanced → DaVinci Resolve + CapCut for quick turnarounds. Professional/client work → Premiere Pro or Final Cut Pro.
- Must-have editor features flagged: auto-captions, fast 9:16 vertical export, no watermark on free tier, speed controls.

**Clipping/bounty platforms:** The articles are written by clipping.net (CLIPPING) itself, so platform comparison content is largely self-promotional. The one concrete, platform-agnostic guidance worth keeping: **red flags to avoid in any clip-bounty platform** — no transparent/real-time view tracking, delayed payments, "get rich quick" promises (e.g. "$10K in your first week"), and any platform charging an upfront fee to join. Legitimate platforms are free to join and pay based on tracked performance.

**Payment methods mentioned as standard for the clipping industry:** PayPal (most common), Cash App, Zelle, bank transfer, and crypto (BTC, ETH, USDT, USDC, Solana/Solana USDC).

**Typical bounty pay-rate bands cited repeatedly (per 100K views):**
| Tier | Rate |
|---|---|
| Entry-level | $10-$30 |
| Standard | $30-$75 |
| Premium | $75-$150 |
| High-paying | $150-$300+ |

Platform-specific rate skew noted: YouTube often pays a premium over TikTok/Instagram on the same campaign (e.g., TikTok $50/100K vs. YouTube $75/100K) due to longer shelf life. Minimum view thresholds to unlock payout are typically 20K, 50K, or 100K depending on campaign — views below the threshold don't count at all, so it's worth prioritizing clips likely to clear the minimum over spreading effort too thin.
